Governance maturity: Early. Harbor Point has strong basics in place, including multi-factor sign-in on company email. AI use has grown around those controls rather than through them. The gaps below are common, and most of them can be closed within 60 days.

The three findings that matter most

1

Customer details are going into personal chatbot accounts.

Four office staff paste service notes, home addresses and billing disputes into their own free chatbot accounts to draft customer replies. The company doesn't own those accounts, can't see what was shared, and can't delete it if an employee leaves.

2

Calls are being recorded without a consent process.

Two managers use an AI note-taker that joins customer and vendor calls and records them. Harbor Point operates in states that require every party's consent to record, and nothing tells callers a recording is running.

3

No one approves, owns or reviews AI tools.

Six of the seven tools arrived without a decision from anyone. Nobody knows which tools are in use, what they cost, or what happens to the data they collect. When something goes wrong, there's no one whose job it is to respond.

Recommended plan

Ninety days, in order of risk

Each step names an owner and a deadline in the full plan. The order follows the risk: stop the exposure first, then write the rules, then make them stick.

First 30 daysStop the exposure
  1. Name an AI owner. The office manager approves every new AI tool and keeps the inventory current.
  2. Move chatbot use to company accounts. Business accounts with data-use settings reviewed. Personal accounts are no longer used for work.
  3. Pause the note-taker on outside calls until there's a consent process that tells every caller a recording is running.
  4. Remove the writing extension from every computer used for banking, payroll or the CRM.
Days 31 to 60Set the rules
  1. Publish the AI use policy and data handling rules, with a one-hour training session for the whole team.
  2. Review the field service AI features and the chat widget contract. Confirm who can see transcripts and how long they're kept. Move the widget contract into Harbor Point's name.
  3. Tighten shared folder access so the email assistant can only reach what each person actually needs.
Days 61 to 90Keep it running
  1. Adopt the vendor review checklist for every new AI tool.
  2. Add AI to the incident response plan, including who calls the customer if an AI tool gets something wrong.
  3. Run the first quarterly access review and re-score.

Sample policy excerpts

What the rules look like on paper

Every review ends with policies written for the business, in plain language the team will actually follow. Two short excerpts from Harbor Point's draft:

AI Use Policy · Section 2

Approved tools

  1. Use only the AI tools listed in Appendix A for company work, and always sign in with your company account.
  2. Never use a personal AI account for work that involves customers, employees or company finances.
  3. To request a new tool, send the AI Tool Request form to the AI owner. Don't sign up first and ask later.
  4. Don't install AI browser extensions on any computer used for banking, payroll or the CRM.
Data Handling Rules · Section 1

What never goes into an AI tool

  • Payment card numbers, bank details, or anyone's Social Security number
  • Customer door codes, alarm codes, lockbox codes or gate codes
  • Employee pay, health, or disciplinary information
  • Anything a customer has asked us to keep private

When in doubt, take out names and addresses first, or ask the AI owner.
